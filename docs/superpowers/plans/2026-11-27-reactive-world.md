# Reactive World Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Pedestrians and passing cars, scripted on simulation time and set off by
the learner's actions. They give mirror and blind-spot checks a visible
consequence in lessons, and add looping background traffic in free drive.

**Architecture:** A pure TypeScript actor module (`src/world/actors.ts`, no
Cannon bodies) advances every actor once per fixed 1/120 s step. It returns:
- actor poses, for rendering;
- the id of an actor touching the learner's footprint, which becomes a
  `collision` failure;
- near-miss events, for the attempt summary;
- the actor boxes the parking sensors should see.

Schedules are data: lesson entries in `scenarios.ts`, ambient loops in
`actor-schedules.ts`. Rendering clones the driven vehicle's GLB (as parked
cars already do) and a pedestrian template. The template is a runtime
placeholder until the generated pedestrian is approved.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript 7, Three.js 0.187, cannon-es 0.20 (untouched by
actors), Vitest 5, Playwright 1.64 (Chromium only).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-27-reactive-world-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- **Determinism:** actor state is a pure function of the schedule, the rules
  and the per-step learner inputs, with dt = 1/120 s. No `Math.random`,
  `Date` or `performance` in actor code.
- **Rules:** `'coach'` for stages 1–2 (actors always stop in time) and
  `'realistic'` for stages 3–5 and free drive.
  - Pedestrians: stopping distance 0.5 m from cruise.
  - Cars: braking at 6 m/s².
- **Speeds:** pedestrian cruise 1.4 m/s. Car cruise 20 km/h (5.56 m/s) on the
  roadside and 10 km/h (2.78 m/s) in the garage. Never more than 30 km/h.
- **Blocking:** an actor stopped by a *stationary* learner (|speed| < 0.2 m/s)
  waits 3 s, then takes its detour. Each lesson actor is gone within 15 s of
  its trigger.
- **Triggers:** each one fires at most once per attempt. An attempt is one
  preview session, so a retry naturally resets the actors.
- **Visible actors:** at most 2 at once in a lesson and 3 in free drive.
  - Main-pass draw calls must stay ≤140 in lessons and ≤100 in the sandbox.
  - The environment stays ≤30,000 triangles.
- **Ids:** pedestrian ids start with `ped-` and car ids with `car-`. An
  actor contact is a `collision` whose `obstacleId` is the actor id.
- **Sensors:** actors moving parallel to the learner (heading within 20°,
  either direction) with their centre more than 1.0 m laterally outside the
  learner's mirror-width footprint are not reported. All others are reported.
- **Assets:** GLBs are generated by `tools/generate-assets.mjs`, never
  hand-edited. The pedestrian stays behind an opt-in generator flag until
  the developer approves it (`docs/asset-pipeline.md`).
- **UI copy:** Vietnamese, in `src/messages.ts` (`vi`).
- **No changes** to stage spawns, targets, regions, steps or pass criteria.
- Commit messages end with the session's Co-Authored-By/Claude-Session
  trailer.

## Deviations from the approved spec (called out for the developer)

- **Stage 5 car trigger** is `reverse-selected`, not `first-stop`. At the
  stage 5 spawn (x = -1.5), the learner's mirror-width footprint
  (left edge x ≈ -2.53) blocks the left lane. A car triggered on the first
  stop would mostly just wait and back off. The stage 5 pedestrian keeps the
  `first-stop` trigger.
- **The sensor lateral threshold is 1.0 m, not the spec's 1.5 m.** With
  1.5 m, the stage 3 passing car (centre x = -2.35, learner edge x = -1.02,
  so 1.33 m outside) would set off the front-left-outer sensor. That breaks
  the "no constant alarms" anti-goal.

## Review Focus

1. **A learner who parks across an actor's path and waits.** The actor must
   wait 3 s, detour, and leave. It must never freeze the lesson or overlap
   the car. Pinned in Task 2 (`stationary learner blocking both path and
   detour is cleared by the lifetime cap`).
2. **Pausing and resuming mid-encounter.** Simulation time stops, so actor
   positions must not jump on resume. Pinned in Task 7 (`actors do not
   advance while paused`).
3. **A learner reversing into an actor that has already stopped, in stage
   1–2.** This must fail as a collision. The coach guard only stops the
   *actor's* motion. Pinned in Task 2 (`coach rules: learner driving into a
   stopped pedestrian is a contact`).
4. **Old attempt-history records** without `nearMisses` must still load,
   and a malformed `nearMisses` must reject that one record. Pinned in Task 4.
5. **A trigger fired twice** (R → D → R, or the indicator toggled
   off and on) must not spawn a second actor. Pinned in Task 1 (`trigger
   fires at most once`).

### Task 2: Yield, braking, near misses, contact and detours

**Files:**
- Modify: `src/world/actors.ts`, `src/world/actors.test.ts`

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: Task 1.
- Produces: the behaviour behind `stepActors`' `contactId` and
  `nearMissIds`, and `ActorPose.braking`.
- Constants, exported for tests:
  - `PEDESTRIAN_STOP_DISTANCE_M = 0.5`
  - `CAR_DECEL_MPS2 = 6`
  - `LOOKAHEAD_MARGIN_M = { pedestrian: 0.5, car: 1.0 }`
  - `COACH_GAP_M = 0.3`
  - `BLOCKED_WAIT_S = 3`
  - `LESSON_LIFETIME_S = 15`
  - `ACCEL_MPS2 = { pedestrian: 1, car: 2 }`

Rules, in order, each step:
1. **Corridor ahead.** The actor's box swept along its path from the current
   distance to distance + stopping distance (v²/(2·decel)) + lookahead
   margin, sampled every 0.05 m. If that sweep overlaps the learner's
   footprint, the actor is "braking for learner". It decelerates at its
   limit; otherwise it accelerates back to cruise.
2. **Coach guard (coach rules only).** Never advance to a distance where the
   actor's box, inflated by `COACH_GAP_M`, overlaps the learner footprint.
   Clamp the advance and set the speed to 0.
3. **Near miss.** When an actor enters "braking for learner" while the
   learner's |speed| > 0.2, emit its id once. The encounter ends when the
   corridor is clear again.
4. **Contact.** If the actor's box (not inflated) overlaps the learner
   footprint after the move, `contactId` is that actor's id. When several
   overlap, take the first in schedule order.
5. **Blocked.** If the actor's speed is < 0.05 and the learner's |speed| is
   < 0.2 continuously for `BLOCKED_WAIT_S`, it switches once to its detour.
   - `turn-back`: the path walked so far, reversed.
   - `offset`: the remaining path shifted by `lateralM` to the left of the
     travel direction, joined by a 2 m lateral transition.

   If it is blocked again on the detour for `BLOCKED_WAIT_S`, or a lesson
   actor reaches `LESSON_LIFETIME_S` since its trigger, it is finished and
   hidden.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests:**
  - `realistic car brakes at 6 m/s²`: car at 5.56 m/s, stationary learner box across the path 10 m ahead. Speed drops by 0.05 m/s per step (6/120, ±1e-9). It stops before contact and `contactId` stays null.
  - `realistic rules allow a late cut-in`: learner footprint placed 1.0 m ahead of a cruising car on the step it appears. Within 0.5 s, `contactId === 'car-…'`.
  - `coach rules never let actor motion cause contact`: the same late cut-in under `'coach'`. `contactId` is null on every step, and the actor's distance does not advance while the guard holds.
  - `coach rules: learner driving into a stopped pedestrian is a contact`: the pedestrian stopped by the guard, then the learner footprint moved 0.4 m into it. `contactId` is the pedestrian id.
  - `near miss emitted once per encounter, only when the learner was moving`: learner moving at 1 m/s into the corridor gives exactly one `nearMissIds` entry. A stationary learner already in the corridor gives none.
  - `stationary blocker → 3 s wait → detour`: for both `turn-back` and `offset` actors, the path switches at 3 s (±1 step), and the actor then finishes without contact.
  - `stationary learner blocking both path and detour is cleared by the lifetime cap`: the actor is hidden no later than trigger + 15 s, and never overlaps the learner.
  - `braking flag mirrors deceleration`: `ActorPose.braking` is true exactly while the actor is "braking for learner".

- [ ] **Step 3: Implement** rules 1–5 in `stepActors`, using `orientedRectangle` and `polygonsOverlap`.
